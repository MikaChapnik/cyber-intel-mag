The cyber-Luddites are coming for AI

T
Tony Glover November 10, 2026 · 2 min read

At the start of the industrial revolution, workers termed "Luddites" banded together to smash machinery that they believed was taking their jobs and destroying their livelihoods. Today, there are growing fears that the vast acres of data centers built to underpin the AI revolution may soon be under attack from similarly motivated cyber-hackers.

Protests erupt across Europe

Protests against data centres and their role in supporting what many see as the socially-destructive technology of AI are already exploding across Europe and the UK. This week, protesters broke into a construction site for a new Microsoft data centre in Amsterdam, clashing with police. Dozens of civil society and grassroots groups have said they will join the "Stop Dirty Data Centres" protests taking place this weekend in 15 European countries, including Ireland, Poland, Denmark, Finland, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, France and the UK. A Community Day of Action is, for example, taking place over the weekend at London's Brick Lane, where a facility is planned on the site of the former Truman Brewery, to highlight the feared impact of AI on jobs and the cost of living.

According to PwC's Global Data Centre Outlook 2026–50, Europe will attract $5.6 trillion in cumulative data centre capital expenditure from 2026 to 2050, accounting for around 18 per cent of total global investment.

AI data centres are increasingly vulnerable

Widespread public concern about the potentially devastating impact of AI on the jobs market is not, however, reflected in governmental attitudes to the new technology. In the UK, for example, Westminster is embracing AI in what many outside parliament see as a naïve belief that AI will eventually boost the country's flagging economy. There are, therefore, growing fears that protesters, many of whom are disgruntled former IT workers recently made redundant by AI, will soon turn from physically attacking to targeting existing AI data centres with targeted cyber-attacks, to which they are increasingly vulnerable.

According to a recent blog, Protecting Against Rising Security Risks in Data Centres, by giant tech conglomerate Cisco: "As the foundation of modern business operations, data centers play a critical role in keeping services running smoothly, which makes them an increasingly attractive target of cyber-attacks."

"AI introduces new cybersecurity vulnerabilities in data centers by expanding the attack surface through interconnected systems and automated workflows. Malicious actors can exploit AI algorithms, training data, or model outputs to bypass security controls or launch targeted attacks," warns Cisco.
